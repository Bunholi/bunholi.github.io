About

0 m About

A first-generation marine scientist from Brazil.

I began my career using molecular tools to detect the illegal trade of sharks and rays (elasmobranchs) and to understand its impact on their conservation. That work showed me how much a single molecule can reveal about the world around us.

Today, most of my research integrates population genomics, environmental DNA, community ecology, and quantitative approaches to study biodiversity across biological scales. I focus especially on cryptobenthic reef fishes, the tiny, hidden species that make up a large share of reef fish diversity.

My long-term goal is to use molecular tools to connect the small- and large-scale patterns that shape eco-evolutionary processes. Linking these biological scales helps reveal how communities form and how they respond to ongoing environmental change.

20 m Research

Biodiversity across biological scales.

Life inside a sponge

The sponge-dwelling goby Risor ruber raises several life stages within a single sponge. I use it as a model for relatedness, dispersal, and social organization, and compare skin and gut microbiomes with the host sponge's to see how a habitat shapes the animal living in it.

ddRAD-seqmicrobiomesocial structure

Understanding fish communities using eDNA

Environmental DNA and RNA let us detect fish from a water sample. Working with Texas Parks and Wildlife, I compare eDNA with long-term fisheries surveys and built the first species-specific assay for southern flounder, testing when eRNA tracks living communities better than eDNA.

eDNA / eRNAqPCR · ddPCRestuaries

Genetic tools for sharks and rays

DNA barcoding and population genetics reveal which threatened species are caught, traded, and eaten, often under misleading names. This line includes the first range-wide genetic study of the angular angelshark and practical rapid-ID tools for monitoring.

DNA barcodingphylogeographywildlife trade

Genomic resources for reef fishes

Molecular monitoring is only as good as its reference data. I co-lead a Brazil-wide reef-fish barcode gap analysis and assembled the first whole genome for the dwarfgoby genus Eviota with Oxford Nanopore sequencing.

Conservation & Outreach

60 m Applied conservation

Research that reaches management.

  • Rapid DNA/eDNA practitioner guide

    Open access · English & Portuguese

    A step-by-step guide that helps fisheries and wildlife-trade monitors identify sharks and rays from samples quickly, published alongside a peer-reviewed toolkit in Molecular Ecology Resources and featured in IUCN Shark News.

  • First southern flounder eDNA assay

    With Texas Parks & Wildlife Department

    The first species-specific primer for Paralichthys lethostigma, integrated with fisheries survey data to evaluate how Texas estuaries are monitored.

  • Angular angelshark population genetics

    Relevant to IUCN reassessment

    The first range-wide genetic study of Squatina guggenheim in the Southwest Atlantic, providing evidence for conservation planning of an endangered endemic species.

  • Brazil reef-fish barcode initiative

    Co-lead · 15+ researchers

    A national gap analysis and reference library combining field expeditions and museum collections, so that eDNA surveys of Brazilian reefs can name what they detect.
